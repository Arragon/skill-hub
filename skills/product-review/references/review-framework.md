# Product Review Framework

Use this reference for comprehensive reviews. Apply only dimensions that can change the decision.

## Stage gates

| Stage | Require before the next commitment |
| --- | --- |
| Idea | Specific recruitable user, triggering problem, current workaround, promised outcome, differentiated insight, falsifiable assumption |
| Validation | Converging evidence from relevant users plus behavioral commitment: time, data, workflow change, pilot, or payment |
| MVP | Narrow end-to-end job, first-value event, repeat-value hypothesis, instrumentation, learning goal, owner for manual work |
| Launch | Positioning, acquisition path, onboarding, support, trust controls, incident handling, success and stop thresholds |
| Growth | Cohort retention, durable channel, plausible unit economics, growth loop, reliability, operational capacity |
| Scale | Governance, resilience, security/compliance, ownership, sustainable economics, controlled complexity |

Do not demand scale evidence from an idea or excuse launch risks as “still early.”

## Product thesis

Write:

> For [specific user] in [triggering situation], who currently [alternative], the product delivers [outcome] through [mechanism] and wins because [meaningful advantage].

Vague terms such as “everyone,” “one-stop,” “intelligent,” “platform,” or “better experience” usually hide an unresolved choice.

## Assumption and evidence map

Record only decision-changing claims. For each, capture type, evidence state, source/recency, limitations, impact if false, confidence, cheapest test, and affected decision.

Use four states:

- **Fact:** independently verifiable or directly observed.
- **Evidence:** relevant signal with known limitations.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible but unsupported.
- **Unknown:** required information is absent.

Prioritize qualitatively by **impact if false × uncertainty × irreversibility**.

Evidence generally strengthens from intuition → repeated unprompted reports → observed workaround/cost → realistic prototype use → commitment of time/data/access/workflow → payment or contract → repeat use/cohort retention → replication across segments. Adapt this ladder for enterprise, regulated, marketplace, public-interest, and safety-critical products.

## Review lenses

| Lens | Decision questions | Common warning signs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User/problem | Who hurts most? What triggers it, how often, and at what cost? Who uses, buys, approves, and benefits? | “Everyone”; solution-defined problem; praise without action |
| Alternatives | What happens today? Why is the workaround good enough? What must users stop or migrate? | “No competitors”; feature-only comparison; ignored switching cost |
| Value/positioning | What result improves, how quickly, and why is it perceptibly better? What tradeoff is accepted? | Feature list as value; several unrelated promises; generic AI claims |
| Workflow/adoption | Where does it enter work? Required data, permissions, integrations, colleagues, setup, and first value? | Value arrives after heavy setup; hidden manual work; unavailable data |
| Retention | What recurring event returns users? What compounds? Is this naturally one-shot? | Opens mistaken for value; novelty or discounts drive return |
| Distribution | Where are users reachable and who has trust? What wedge, channel, or product loop wins first? | “Use social media”; unsupported virality; community replacing focus |
| Economics | Who pays, from which budget, for what unit of value? Which costs scale with usage/support/risk? | High variable cost with postponed revenue; copied pricing; heroic conversion |
| Feasibility | Hardest dependency? Minimum useful quality? Smallest end-to-end slice? Can demand be tested manually? | Demo treated as production; component roadmap; uncontrolled dependency |
| Operations | Who owns exceptions, support, moderation, disputes, quality, and incidents? Does service burden fit pricing? | Unowned queues; bespoke service at self-serve economics |
| Trust/risk | Harms from error, misuse, leakage, bias, or automation? Need explanation, appeal, review, audit, or current legal verification? | “AI decides”; excessive sensitive data; security equated with compliance |
| Measurement | What proves first and repeated value? What guardrail and stop condition prevent metric gaming? | Vanity metrics; post-hoc success criteria; experiment cannot alter roadmap |
| Defensibility | What compounds—workflow position, trust, lawful data rights, network, capability, or channel? Can it survive response? | Complexity as moat; data without rights; network effect before single-player value |

## Constructive grill

Ask only if the answer changes diagnosis, priority, experiment, or verdict. Question order:

1. What decision is being made now?
2. Which unsupported assumption could invalidate it?
3. What was observed, from whom, and under what conditions?
4. What do users do today and why would they switch?
5. What real commitment proves importance?
6. What constraints or external dependencies are fixed?
7. What evidence would trigger a change or stop?

Push back with:

> The plan depends on [claim]. Current material is [evidence state], insufficient for [commitment]. If false, [consequence]. Test/change [action], and continue only if [signal].

Reject constructively:

> I do not recommend proceeding at the current scope because [critical dependency] is unresolved. Preserve [value], narrow to [path], and first validate [assumption].

Do not stack rhetorical questions, repeat answered questions, treat uncertainty as failure, attack the user, or continue interrogating after the decision is clear.

## Priorities and verdicts

Classify findings as fatal now, critical before next commitment, important, later, or distraction.

Choose one verdict:

- **Proceed:** evidence supports the proposed next step.
- **Proceed with constraints:** continue within explicit limits and checkpoints.
- **Validate before building:** demand/problem evidence is too weak for implementation.
- **Narrow or reposition:** value exists but user, use case, promise, or scope is diffuse.
- **Rework a core assumption:** evidence or constraints conflict with the model.
- **Pause:** a critical dependency cannot yet be resolved responsibly.
- **Do not proceed in the current form:** commitment is unjustified; state what could make a revised version viable.

Always state confidence and upgrade/downgrade evidence.

## Validation design

Test the riskiest assumption, not the easiest feature. Define: assumption; method; participant/environment; sample rationale; real commitment; success and failure signals; time/cost box; false-positive/negative risks; decision rule.

Prefer observed past behavior, workflow observation, concierge/Wizard-of-Oz delivery, realistic prototype tasks, ethical fake doors, pilot/data/calendar commitments, real pricing conversations, and cohort retention. Match evidence strength to risk; do not prescribe a universal sample size.

## Scorecard and failure controls

Score only when requested or useful: 0 contradicted/absent; 1 assertion; 2 plausible with weak signal; 3 relevant evidence; 4 repeated behavioral/outcome evidence. Mark N/A and show evidence. Never average away a fatal issue.

Avoid framework dumping, premature features or scaling, false certainty, empty praise, performative harshness, jargon-driven judgment, vague advice, hidden value tradeoffs, and refusing to update when new evidence arrives.
